All times are America/Chicago (CDT, UTC−5) and are exact instants; an **estimated** end is information, never a confirmed reopening, and nothing in the app reopens a closure because a date passed.

Three things are kept apart everywhere:

## The gate (what "rides the closed section" means)

The first check required 15 m of accumulated proximity and let short saved routes wholly inside the Willow interval (5, 10 and 14 m) start. It is replaced by positive travel along the section, with numerical noise only:

- **Travel is decided on continuous runs.** A route polyline is judged as a whole: each maximal run of consecutive legs that lie on the line (both ends within 0.5 m of it, aligned within about 26 degrees) sums its overlap with the closed interval, and a run counts when that sum exceeds 0.01 m (numerical noise). A leg contributes only its own overlap, so geometry outside the interval can never hide travel inside it (an outside bend within the 0.5 m geometry tolerance, then an exact closed tail, is refused), and the answer cannot depend on how many collinear vertices a geometry keeps or how the route groups it into segments and edges (consecutive polylines that meet are one geometry): 0.01 m subdivisions, 1000-point copies, two- and three-segment regroupings all get the sparse answer, in both directions, after a reload, at both bounds. A repeated vertex has no direction and neither continues nor breaks a run.
- **There is no error allowance and no reach.** The line a leg is judged against is the line it lies on, and the interval on that line is the closure's own. On the unchanged source line that is the stored section. A saved route that follows a graph-node-anchored chord is judged on that chord.
- **Source-to-derived correspondence comes from the CURRENT graph, never from the saved route.** The graph puts every vertex within its 15 m snap tolerance on one node, and snapped or junction geometry is drawn on those node points: a source leg whose end vertex was moved onto a neighboring feature's node (a spur listed first, 14 m away in the worst case, 13.8 m on the actual reordered network) becomes a leg off the source line. `TrailRouteClosureDerivationSijko.legsFor(graph)` walks the graph's own edges (runs with their node-anchored form, and junction connectors with their raw counterpart) and, piecewise, pairs each raw leg with the derived leg of the same index (only the moved end vertices replaced by their anchors). Where the closure's bounds (or crossing) lie ON a raw leg, the part of the interval that overlaps that raw leg is carried onto its derived leg by its fraction along that raw leg, exactly (a junction connector's raw counterpart can cover only part of the source leg, so the interval is intersected with the leg, not required to lie inside it: a bound behind the connector's projection clamps to the connector's start): a route that rides 0.02 m past the carried bound is refused, and one that ends exactly at it is open (a multi-vertex run is paired leg by leg, not by whole-polyline arc length). The bridge builds these from the uncut graph of the route's own layer choices (`webBridge`, cached with the revalidation index) and passes them to the gate; nothing about them is serialized or trusted from a saved route, and the existing revalidation already requires every drawn line to derive from the current graph or the route is stale (moved or redrawn source geometry makes a saved route stale). This is not a blanket radius: other features, the spur itself and mapped roads are never judged by it.
- A perpendicular crossing (the actual Cypress feature meets the trail 0.4 m from the south bound), a junction hop, and an approach that ends exactly at a bound have no leg on the line that overlaps the interval, so they are not travel along the section. There is no minimum riding distance.
- Only trail legs are considered: a mapped access road can inherit the enclosing trail edge's source id, and is never classified as the closed trail from that id.
- The crossing blocker uses the same provenance but tests passing through a point instead of an interval: a leg within 3 m of the crossing point, or a leg on a derived leg that spans the crossing's carried position (3 m touching tolerance). Any leg of a polyline counts, so subdivision cannot change it.
- **Recalculation** cannot launder a route out of a closed section. A saved route whose start or destination lies inside the Willow section has no way around it: the only geometry a replacement can have is an unrouted, estimated hop to the nearest bound and back, which is not a detour. The shared gate (`TrailRouteClosureGateSijko.recalculate`) therefore refuses a replacement that travels a closure or has an ESTIMATED (unrouted) hop that starts, ends or travels inside a closed section (`TrailRouteAdvisorySijko.entersClosedSection`; an estimated hop has no source, so it is judged with the graph's own 15 m reach), so no front end is handed an ungated "rerouted around the closure". A routed segment is never judged by its vertices: a mapped open road that crosses the trail inside the section is an ordinary road whether or not it keeps a collinear vertex there, so an eligible mapped detour is offered in both representations (tested sparse and dense). The web's separate estimated-gap rule is unchanged and is not what the shared gate relies on. Unaffected routes (residual approaches that stop at a bound) still recalculate to a replacement.
- **Unavailable correspondence fails closed.** The gate's `derived` argument has three meanings: a list from the graph (exact transfer), an empty list (the graph was checked and has none), and null, the default, which says the correspondence is UNAVAILABLE. The shared callers that have no graph (the Android screen, the share text) pass null. With null the gate no longer reasons about alignment or about whether a leg is on the unchanged line (an anchor can move along the line as well as across it, and opposite anchors rotate a short leg), because none of that proves the edge unmoved. It uses the one guarantee the graph gives: it moves any vertex by at most its 15 m snap reach, so every point of a carried interval, being a combination of anchors each within that reach of its source vertex, is within 15 m of the raw interval, and likewise for the crossing. A leg of the closure's own feature (or an unattributed trail leg) that comes within 15 m of the closed section (leg to section distance) or of the crossing point therefore counts as riding it. Legs of other features, and legs farther than that, are not judged by it. The price is deliberate and stated: with no graph, travel on the closure's own feature within 15 m of a closed section or crossing is refused, INCLUDING an approach that ends exactly at a bound; the exact rule above applies to a caller that supplies the correspondence. `TrailRouteClosureGateSijko.recalculate` always has the network, so it computes the correspondence itself (the uncut graph for the saved route, the graph it just planned on for the replacement) and never falls back. No native UI changed and no caller was made to load the network; native is not a web acceptance item and no device was exercised.
- **Conservative edge, stated:** on a network where the graph moved a node, a tap on the PHYSICAL (raw) bound can land on the chord away from the carried bound, and the raw line and the carried interval are ORed, so an approach planned to that tap can be refused where the carried interval alone would open it (about 3 m was seen on the actual reordered network, where the chord is the raw leg stretched toward a node 13.8 m away; that figure is from that one network and an east-shift control, not a bound). The refusing side is the safe side; exact bound-ending controls use the carried bounds and are tested only for the east-shifted spur fixtures.

## Saved routes and Recalculate

A saved route is checked again every time it is opened or restored, against the loaded network and the current instant. When a closure affects it the preview shows the current notice (the Scheduled or the in-force text), keeps Start off, and offers **Recalculate around the closure**. The saved record is not touched by opening, restoring or recalculating it.

- **Recalculate** asks the real core for a freshly checked route and shows it as a **temporary** preview ("Recalculated route · not saved"). Nothing is written to the library: not the original, not Recent, not the result. Start accepts it only through the existing gates (a route with an unverified connection, for example, still cannot start and says so, and is never announced as ready).
- **Save as new route** is the only way in. The route's identity is minted once when the recalculation succeeds (the geometry key plus a nonce), so saving it can never overwrite the route it came from or another saved route that happens to share the geometry, and saving the same temporary route twice is the same record. The geometry key is kept beside it (`geometryKey`) because a reversal is checked against geometry, not identity. There is no Replace.
- **Status survives a reload.** The session keeps `temporary`, the identity, `geometryKey`, the original (`recalculatedFrom`) and a reversed loop's planned direction (`plannedKey` with `plannedGeometryKey`), so a recalculated loop that is reversed, reloaded and reversed back is still the same temporary route and saves as the planned direction. Restoring writes nothing. Records saved before this change are untouched and keep their geometry key as their identity; there is no library migration.
- **A restored direction that is still being proved waits.** After a reload the route on screen is the REVERSED record until the planned direction is proved by geometry, so while that proof is pending Save, the Saved/View indicator and Reverse are unavailable for that route (a note says why); a Save could otherwise store the reversed geometry key instead of the planned identity, or show a different saved route as this one. A proof that cannot be established shows the route as itself, and a temporary one then gets an identity of its own minted once, so it can never save under a geometry key that belongs to another saved route. A late proof also never undoes a status the rider settled in the meantime. Held-response regressions cover the pending window, the collision (the reversed geometry already saved), success and the unproved case.
- **No safe route, or another gate blocks it:** the error says so and that the saved route is unchanged; the route on screen is inspected again before the operation ends, so Start eligibility is never left over from before.
- **Ownership:** a recalculation, its failure inspection and its finally block are all checked against the operation token, so an answer that returns after the rider left, cancelled or opened another route adopts nothing and writes nothing. A storage failure on Save announces no success, keeps the route temporary and leaves the library as it was.

Evidence: the closure controls run against the real core (`webApp/tests/county/saved-recalculate.spec.ts`: the page clock is moved and the app passes its own clock to the worker, on synthetic data that reuses the actual Willow source leg), the identity, direction and ownership controls run in the fixture suite (`webApp/tests/e2e/rider.spec.ts`), and the store and session rules are unit tests (`webApp/tests/unit/recalculated-route.test.ts`). Rendering fixtures elsewhere do not prove gating.

- Help no longer says the county data has no street or shared-roadway information or that the app has no road network: it counts existing features only (proposed ones are said apart), says how many are shared roadways, and when the loaded record has road access it describes it and says routing also uses it.
