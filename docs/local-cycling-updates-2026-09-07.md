## Rules: distinguish current requirements from January 2027 changes

Illinois **SB 3484/Public Act 104-0854 was signed August 26, 2026, and takes effect January 1, 2027**. It adds a minimum age of 15 for Class 1/2 e-bike operators; Class 3 remains 16. It also restricts passengers carried by operators under 18 and addresses higher-powered devices that do not qualify as low-speed e-bikes, including their exclusion from bicycle/shared-use paths. Do not apply the new minimum-age requirement early. [Secretary of State announcement](https://www.ilsos.gov/news/2026/august-26-2026-giannoulias-landmark-micromobility-safety-legislation-signed-into-law.html), [enacted text](https://ilga.gov/ftp/Public%20Acts/104/104-0854.htm), [bill status/effective date](https://www.ilga.gov/Legislation/BillStatus?DocNum=3484&DocTypeID=SB&GA=104&GAID=18&SessionID=114).

Normal's existing official table explicitly allows **e-bikes on Constitution Trail and streets, but not sidewalks**; it does not distinguish classes. Trail hours are one hour before sunrise to one hour after sunset, and riders must use a safe speed and signal before passing. No numeric speed limit appears on that page. [Normal Rules of the Trail](https://www.normalil.gov/1467/Rules-of-the-Trail).

Bloomington's park ordinance sets **15 mph** for vehicles, subject to its designated-road exception, and defines bicycles as vehicles and recreational trails as parks. Applying that limit to Bloomington trail sections follows those definitions; it should not be generalized to Normal as a shared numeric rule. [Traffic ordinance](https://ecode360.com/34411375), [definitions](https://ecode360.com/34411338).

The old Bloomington motorized-vehicle prohibition does not expressly address modern qualifying low-speed e-bikes. No new 2025–2026 local e-bike ban was confirmed. Do not equate generic “motorized vehicles prohibited” trail text with a verified ban on every e-bike. Device-specific legality is not determined in this research.

## Map findings and Trail Mapper implications

Friends of Constitution Trail identifies the [McLean County GIS map](https://mcleangis.maps.arcgis.com/apps/instant/sidebar/index.html?appid=d98c151296fd4b03860af8f4df7787a4) as its most current map. Its downloadable [paper brochure](https://www.constitutiontrail.org/_files/ugd/05629c_44a060ef89384409a6858e66a02a2cac.pdf) explicitly says **Updated: June 2022**. A recent crawl or search-result date does not make it a 2026 map. [Official map links](https://www.constitutiontrail.org/trail-maps).

A fresh extraction of the two county routing layers on September 7, 2026, was compared field-by-field and geometry-by-geometry with the source packaged in the installed APK:

- 65 Constitution Trail Branches and 195 Other Trails: **260 total**.
- **Zero added, removed or changed normalized features** compared with the July 9, 2026 extraction.
- The APK's trail asset matches that packaged source exactly.

This verifies the app matches those two live layers; it does not prove the county map captures every recent opening or temporary closure. The city's capital-project layer had underlying data edits as recently as **September 4, 2026**, distinct from its older dashboard configuration date.

The clearest app opportunities from this research are temporary closure information separate from base geometry, explicit project/opening status, and jurisdiction-aware e-bike/rule information with effective dates. Newly funded or proposed corridors should stay out of default usable routing. No app routing data or code was changed during this research.
